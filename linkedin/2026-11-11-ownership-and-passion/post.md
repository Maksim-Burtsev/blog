Teams are cutting code review faster than they're asking who writes the code.

Reviewing less works in one case: when the team is people with ownership and passion.

These engineers want the result to be good, not just done. For them work is a big part of life, not something that ends at five. If something breaks in the evening, they jump in without questions and fix what they shipped.

Their name is next to the code, so they feel directly responsible for it, even when formally their manager is.

Without such people, agents make most of the decisions and nobody checks them. Slop piles up in the codebase, and nothing on the outside shows it.

The more code agents write, the more the focus shifts from the code to the people behind it.
