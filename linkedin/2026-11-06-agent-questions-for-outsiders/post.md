When you forward your agent’s questions to colleagues as they are, you become a proxy.

You have context the agent doesn’t, and at least for now, it won’t fit in the agent’s context. But you skip yourself in the chain and just move the work from your agent to another person.

And your colleagues get the extra work. LLM text is low density, so they read several times more than if you’d written it. And many of the questions don’t need them at all: in my experience, 20–30% already have an answer in the code, the task description or the system.

That’s disrespect, and people see it. A reputation you built over years goes fast, however strong you actually are.

What your agent gives you is raw. What your team gets from you should be processed: a few lines that are short, dense and to the point.
